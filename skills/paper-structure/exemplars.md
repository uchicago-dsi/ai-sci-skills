# Exemplar papers

Papers the author has pointed to as writing worth imitating, with what each one
teaches about the order of an argument. Read this before laying out a new paper
or restructuring a draft. The lessons are about explanation order and how a
result is connected to its question; none of them licenses a stronger claim
than the evidence supports.

Source: the [Hendren showcase](https://www.hendrenwriting.com/showcase), a
collection the author supplied as examples of clear scientific writing. A
collection is not an endorsement of every stylistic choice in every paper in
it, so record which paper a lesson came from and add a paper only after
reading it.

## Epel et al. 2004, "Accelerated telomere shortening in response to life stress"

[PMC534658](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC534658/). Read: abstract,
Introduction, Results, Discussion.

- Opens on a problem the reader already recognises, then says exactly what is
  not known about it.
- Explains an unfamiliar measurement at the point the question first needs it,
  not in a preliminary glossary.
- States why each comparison was made before giving its number.
- Keeps every result attached to the question it answers, so a reader never
  holds a number without knowing what it was for.
- Says what an association supports and what further evidence would establish
  the mechanism. Clear writing does not buy causal language.

## The et al. 2000

[PMC27539](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC27539/). Read: abstract,
Introduction, Methods, opening Results.

- Opens on an observable discrepancy, gives one concrete example of it, and
  says why it matters, all before describing the investigation.
- The concrete case makes the problem understandable before any coefficient
  appears. An illustrative case must be labelled as one and followed by the
  cohort evidence.

## The order these imply

For an Introduction:

1. The measurement or quantity the reader cares about, and what it depends on.
2. What goes wrong with it, ideally as one concrete, observable discrepancy.
3. The specific question that remains unresolved.
4. How this study answers it, and what it will show.

Technical identities, derivations and caveats that the reader does not need to
follow this line move to Methods or Discussion, where they serve a step.

For each Results subsection: the question in a sentence, the reason for the
comparison that answers it, the finding in words, then the deciding numbers
with their denominators.

## Adding an exemplar

Read the paper before recording it, say which sections were read, and write
lessons as what the paper does, not as adjectives about it. A lesson that
cannot be pointed to in the text is a preference, and belongs in the project's
own voice notes until the author confirms it.
